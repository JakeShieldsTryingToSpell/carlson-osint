# Richard Warner Carlson — research notes

[Summary](README.md) · [Evidence and sources](evidence/README.md) · [Source index](evidence/sources.md)

Public-source research imported November 27, 2026. Each passage retains its source attribution. The outstanding questions identify what would change the finding.

## Key findings

- Nine original FDD tax returns document **$1,002,124 in compensation and $110,455 in benefits or other compensation** for Richard Carlson from 2003 through 2011. Seven returns document **$2,098,273 in contractor payments and expense reimbursements** to his company, Tulip Hill Enterprises, from 2005 through 2011. In 2004, Richard and Patricia Carlson also **gave FDD $60,000**. [FDD returns index](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/134174402) · [2004 return, PDF p. 17](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/display_990/134174402/2005_11_PF%2F13-4174402_990PF_200412)
- FDD disclosed another **$119,750 to Circle 40 in 2011** for grant-supported radio and event expenses. It identified Carlson in the related-party entry but gave no corporate name expansion, officer list, project sponsor or ownership details. The same filing recorded **$324,499** for Tulip Hill. [2011 return, Schedule L, PDF p. 37](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/display_990/134174402/2012_12_EO%2F13-4174402_990_201112)
- **Tulip Hill Enterprises Incorporated** was chartered in Virginia in 1998. The state-data record lists Richard W. Carlson as president, Patricia Carlson as vice president and automatic termination in 2023. The name occurs in tax and election records and, in variant forms, in documentary and publishing credits. [Virginia SCC entity 05113022](https://cis.scc.virginia.gov/EntitySearch/BusinessInformation?businessId=05113022) · [Virginia SCC data mirror](https://vabusinesses.org/business/05113022) · [FEC, 2014](https://docquery.fec.gov/pdf/724/14940547724/14940547724.pdf)
- Tucker Carlson publicly described his father working with the CIA in 2024. In 2025 he said that after his father's death he learned of involvement in the intelligence world and was surprised. Buckley later denied direct employment. Dick himself discussed intelligence matters and a classified satellite-tracking facility in his diplomatic oral history. **The public records reviewed do not establish CIA employment, a contract, a station assignment or a named covert operation involving Dick Carlson.** [Shawn Ryan interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uElaHUSM7fI&t=3050s) · [August 2025 interview](https://singjupost.com/transcript-candace-owens-interview-on-the-tucker-carlson-show/) · [Buckley interview](https://fight.fudgie.org/search/show/cha/episode/20261120_Fri__4SQgLfo6_E) · [Carlson oral history](https://adst.org/OH%20TOCs/CARLSON%2C%20Richard%20W.toc.pdf)
- The best documented VOA controversies were **Radio Martí's reassignment of a reporter over a Nicaragua question**, **Bruce Gelb's efforts to steer China coverage**, and **a fight over accusations of pro-Iraqi bias in Arabic broadcasts**. In Seychelles, Carlson described coordinating Western pressure for multiparty politics, representing U.S. interests around the tracking station and reporting an alleged connection between the embassy landlord and BCCI. [Radio Martí reporting](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1986-11-21-mn-14715-story.html) · [USIA history](https://universityofleeds.github.io/philtaylorpapers/pmt/exhibits/3197/CullUSIAdemise.pdf) · [Carlson oral history](https://adst.org/OH%20TOCs/CARLSON%2C%20Richard%20W.toc.pdf) · [1992 election assessment](https://www.ifes.org/sites/default/files/migrate/r01814.pdf)
- A later conflict-of-interest question rests on an unusually direct record. **Policy Impact**, where Dick served as a director, reported arranging Hungarian Foreign Minister Péter Szijjártó's interview on Tucker's show as work for the Hungarian embassy. The filing identifies the work and the firm. Carlson's individual participation and any personal compensation remain unknown. [DOJ supplemental filing, item 11](https://efile.fara.gov/docs/6544-Supplemental-Statement-20190515-1.pdf) · [DOJ contract exhibit](https://efile.fara.gov/docs/6544-Exhibit-AB-20190515-3.pdf) · [Átlátszó reporting](https://atlatszo.hu/kozpenz/2023/11/15/szazmilliokat-kolt-amerikai-lobbizasra-a-magyar-kormany-twitter-vitakra-es-tucker-carlson-riportra-is-jutott-penz/)

## Identity and timeline

Richard Warner Carlson, commonly Richard W. Carlson or Dick Carlson, was born in Boston on February 10, 1941, and died March 24, 2025. The contemporaneous records match him by title, employer and career history. Same-name donor and company records without corroborating identifiers were excluded. The entries for 2003 and 2004 indicate years without an identified Tulip Hill contractor payment in this review. The 2004 return's **Schedule B contributor no. 16** does identify Richard and Patricia together as **$60,000 donors to FDD**. The return reports aggregate contributions under the joint entry. It gives no split between them or stated purpose for the gift. [2004 return, PDF p. 17](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/display_990/134174402/2005_11_PF%2F13-4174402_990PF_200412) · [2005 return, PDF p. 10](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/display_990/134174402/2006_12_EO%2F13-4174402_990_200512) · [2011 return, PDF p. 9](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/display_990/134174402/2012_12_EO%2F13-4174402_990_201112)

**Two figures in 2005.** Schedule A names Tulip Hill at **$385,386**. Audited financial statement note 7 reports **approximately $410,000** paid for media production services to a company in which FDD's vice chairman was a principal, with **approximately $105,000 in future commitments** as of December 31. The named line and approximate audit figure differ by about **$24,614**. The records reviewed give no reconciliation. The table uses the named contractor line and does not add the audit figure or the future commitment. FDD separately reports **$360,833** in program expenses for the *Danger Zone* radio show and **$448,279** for a broader category combining elite outreach and PBS documentary development. Those are program figures with a different scope. FDD was using a modified cash basis in its financial statements. [2005 return, PDF pp. 10, 22, 25–26, 29–36](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/display_990/134174402/2006_12_EO%2F13-4174402_990_200512) · [Contemporaneous reporting rounded to $385,000](https://mondoweiss.net/2008/01/when-it-comes-t/)

**Circle 40.** FDD entered **$119,750** in 2011 Schedule L beside Circle 40 and described grant-supported reimbursements for radio and event expenses. The relationship column says Carlson was vice chair and a former board member. The filing lists Circle 40 and Tulip Hill on separate lines, with “No” in the revenue-sharing column for both. Tulip Hill and Circle 40 together account for **$444,249** of disclosed 2011 transactions. The reviewed 2008–2010 Schedule L entries identify Tulip Hill alone. Exact-name and variant searches for Circle 40 with Carlson, Cowan, FDD and *Danger Zone* yielded no reliable company identification. The FDD entry gives a lead for contracts, canceled checks and expense backup. [2008 Schedule L](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/display_990/134174402/2010_01_EO%2F13-4174402_990_200812) · [2009 Schedule L](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/display_990/134174402/2010_12_EO%2F13-4174402_990_200912) · [2010 Schedule L](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/display_990/134174402/2011_12_EO%2F13-4174402_990_201012) · [2011 Schedule L](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/display_990/134174402/2012_12_EO%2F13-4174402_990_201112)

**2012 follow-up.** The next return does not list Carlson in Part VII, Tulip Hill or Circle 40 in its five highest-paid contractor rows, or either entity in Schedule L Part IV. The return discloses eight independent contractors above $100,000 but names only five there. A payment below a disclosure threshold or outside the listed categories could escape this check. [2012 return, PDF pp. 8–9 and 37](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/display_990/134174402/2013_12_EO%2F13-4174402_990_201212)

**Governance and money trail.** FDD acknowledged the relationship, first calling Carlson a principal and later listing him as Tulip Hill's president or owner. Its 2011 Schedule O describes annual conflict disclosures and reporting potential conflicts to senior leadership. It does not show who approved individual Tulip Hill or Circle 40 transactions, whether Carlson recused himself, whether FDD obtained bids, who made the project grant, or what portion reached any individual. FDD's tax reporting separately identifies personal compensation and corporate receipts. Corporate receipts may cover other production costs, and neither source provides a profit figure or a payroll ledger. Public donor reporting documents funding of FDD as a whole. A grant-specific restriction, contract or accounting entry is necessary to connect an individual donor to Tulip Hill. [2005 relationship statement](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/display_990/134174402/2006_12_EO%2F13-4174402_990_200512) · [2008 Schedule L](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/display_990/134174402/2010_01_EO%2F13-4174402_990_200812) · [2011 Schedules L and O](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/display_990/134174402/2012_12_EO%2F13-4174402_990_201112) · [FDD donor reporting](https://archive.thinkprogress.org/exclusive-documents-shed-light-on-those-underwriting-the-foundation-for-defense-of-democracies-80976d047d1a/)

### Was VOA a CIA outlet?

During Carlson's tenure, VOA was a federal broadcaster within the **U.S. Information Agency**. Its 1976 statutory charter required reliable, objective news, a representative account of American views, and clear presentation and discussion of U.S. policy. An overt government policy function was built into the same mission as objective reporting. During an earlier era, the U.S. government covertly financed **Radio Free Europe and Radio Liberty** through the CIA. A declassified 1971 memorandum discusses ending that arrangement and changing their funding. It concerns those broadcasters specifically. The public organizational record places Carlson in USIA/VOA. [VOA charter](https://www.usagm.gov/who-we-are/oversight/legislation/voa-charter/) · [Congressional Research Service on U.S. international broadcasting](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R46968/R46968.1.pdf) · [Declassified 1971 memorandum](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v29/d52) · [White House nomination](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/nomination-richard-w-carlson-be-united-states-ambassador-the-seychelles)

Public-source searches used Richard W. Carlson, Richard Warner Carlson and Dick Carlson with CIA, CIA Reading Room, USIA, Seychelles, FBIS and *Danger Zone*. No agency-issued statement confirming a CIA employment or contractual relationship was located. The historical record does establish an intelligence-adjacent diplomatic assignment, access to officials in the national security establishment and his son's acknowledgment of unspecified collaboration. [Carlson oral history](https://adst.org/OH%20TOCs/CARLSON%2C%20Richard%20W.toc.pdf) · [Shawn Ryan interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uElaHUSM7fI&t=3050s) · [State Department career record](https://history.state.gov/departmenthistory/people/carlson-richard-w)

## Voice of America, 1986–1991

### Appointment and day-to-day remit

Carlson joined Charles Wick's USIA as public liaison director in 1985, moved to VOA in 1986 and stayed under George H. W. Bush. He recalled attending a lunch arranged by **Roy Cohn and Edwin Feulner** with Wick and conservative figures or staff. The purpose, as Carlson described it, was to win support for a different candidate. He said he subsequently emerged as a candidate himself. His oral history places Cohn in the vetting process, with no independent account of Cohn alone securing his appointment. The Senate indexed Carlson's nomination in 1986 and later indexed hearings involving him on funding and China. [Carlson oral history, appointment discussion](https://adst.org/OH%20TOCs/CARLSON%2C%20Richard%20W.toc.pdf) · [Obituary](https://www.washingtonpost.com/obituaries/2025/03/27/dick-carlson-tucker-voa-dies/) · [1986 Congressional Record](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRI-1986/html/CRI-1986-CARLSON-RICHARD-W.htm) · [1987 Congressional Record](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRI-1987/html/CRI-1987-CARLSON-RICHARD-W.htm) · [1989 Congressional Record](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRI-1989/html/CRI-1989-CARLSON-RICHARD-W.htm)

He oversaw VOA's multilingual services. VOA credits his tenure with establishing a Creole service. By his account, his remit also included Radio Martí, the legislatively authorized beginnings of TV Martí and USIA television later in his tenure. He hosted *On the Line*, a weekly forum designed to explain and debate aspects of U.S. foreign policy. A surviving June 22, 1991 script identifies him as host for a program on minority rights. The VOA biography describes the program as openly policy-oriented. ### Earlier documented controversy

In 1969 Carlson and Lance Brisson co-wrote a *Look* article alleging connections between San Francisco mayor Joseph Alioto and organized crime. A federal court awarded Alioto $350,000 against the publisher over specific allegations, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed in 1980. The opinion is useful when assessing uncorroborated charges repeated from old Carlson reporting or modern commentary. The publisher was the defendant. Carlson also received a Peabody with other KABC staff for later investigative reporting about the Dale car scheme.
