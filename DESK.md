## Profiles and broader research

Choose a person in the [family directory](people/README.md). Add what a reader should know about that person’s own life or public work. For a private relative, a modest, well-sourced entry may be complete.
- **[Do more research on a person or event](https://github.com/JakeShieldsTryingToSpell/carlson-osint/issues?q=is%3Aissue+is%3Aopen+label%3A%22open+research%22).** These assignments leave the angle to the researcher. Read the specific assignments first and post the source or question you are taking before starting. A single sourced advance completes a pass.

## Choosing priorities

**P1** covers a specific decision about money, access, public authority or disclosure that could materially change the archive, and independent reviews that gate a consequential finding. **P2** covers substantive follow-up, promising first profiles and new angles on the main cases. **P3** covers remaining profile foundations, context and lower-impact checks. Within a tier the dispatcher offers eligible reviews first, then the oldest available assignment. A contributor can also choose a particular profile or case.

When opening a new issue, use `Research:`, `Profile:`, `Connection:` or `Review:` and a priority in the title. Apply the existing case or profile label so the work appears above. Link a person, notebook and starting source. For open research, identify a place to start and leave room for a better angle. Give every issue a stopping point, and check open and closed work for duplicates. [Agent instructions](AGENTS.md) · [Document intake](documents/README.md)
